How many calories are in your food?

Search more than 1,000 West African foods, mostly ingredients such as yam, plantain, beans, gari and melon seed, and see calories, protein, fat, carbohydrates, vitamins and minerals. All values are per 100 grams, and you can enter your own portion size to see the numbers for what you actually eat.

Where this data comes from. All nutrient values come from the FAO/INFOODS Food Composition Table for Western Africa (WAFCT 2019), published by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations. This page is a free, non-commercial reference tool and is not endorsed by FAO.

What is and is not included. The table is strongest on raw and plainly cooked ingredients, such as grains, tubers, vegetables, legumes, meat and fish. Some foods are listed under their international names, so we match common Nigerian names for you, for example egusi finds melon seed and ogbono finds dikanut. The recipes in the table are mostly from Burkina Faso, so many Nigerian dishes, such as jollof rice and egusi soup, are not in this version.

Nigerian data filter. The "Includes Nigerian data" filter shows foods where at least one of the sources behind the values was a Nigerian study or table. Some of these values are averages of Nigerian and non-Nigerian sources, so it is a guide and not a guarantee that every number comes from Nigeria.

How to read the numbers. Values are averages per 100 g of the edible portion, as described in the food name. Dishes marked with an asterisk in the name are calculated from recipes, so your own recipe may differ. Italic values are less certain, and "trace" means the nutrient was detected only in very small amounts. A blank means no data is available for that nutrient.

Not medical advice. This tool gives general food composition data. If you are managing a health condition, speak to a registered dietitian or doctor.

Source: Vincent, A., Grande, F., Compaoré, E., et al. 2020. FAO/INFOODS Food Composition Table for Western Africa (2019). Rome, FAO. Built by Wajeun.
